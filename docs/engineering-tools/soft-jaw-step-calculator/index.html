How soft jaw boring works

Soft jaws are bored in the chuck they will be used in (to run true). Two diameters are machined:

  • Main bore: sized to the part OD with a small clearance (typically +0.02–0.05 mm).
  • Step (back-cut / relief): a smaller diameter behind the main bore. The part butts against this shoulder for axial location. The radial step depth is typically 0.5–1.5 mm.

The step height (axial engagement) should be at least 1× the radial depth, and 2–3 mm is typical for gripping strength. Bore the soft jaws at the same clamping pressure and chuck position they will run at in production.

Important: Always bore soft jaws at the approximate clamping diameter you will use in production. If you bore them at a different jaw opening, they will not be concentric. Use a boring bar or the existing chuck bore tool.

Tip:

The main bore is typically cut 0.02–0.05 mm larger than the part diameter for easy loading. The step diameter is then 2 × step_radial_depth smaller. For thin-wall parts, reduce clamping pressure and increase jaw contact area to prevent distortion.
