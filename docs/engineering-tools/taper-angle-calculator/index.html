Formula and use

The taper is defined by the diameter change over length:

  • Included angle: α = 2·atan((D − d) / (2·L))
  • D = d + 2·L·tan(α/2)
  • d = D − 2·L·tan(α/2)
  • L = (D − d) / (2·tan(α/2))
  • Taper per 100 mm = (D − d) / L × 100

On a lathe, the compound rest is swivelled to the half-angle (α/2), not the included angle. When programming a CNC lathe, the toolpath uses the half-angle as well. Standard tapers (Morse, Metric 1:50, etc.) are defined by their taper ratio, not the angle.

Common uses: turning tapered shafts, boring tapered holes, setting up taper-turning attachments, checking Morse taper dimensions, and verifying toolholder tapers (BT/CAT).

Note:

Enter any three of the four fields; the fourth is calculated automatically. If you enter all four, they must agree. Length L is the axial length over which the taper changes from d to D. Results: diameters and length to 3 decimals, angles to 2 decimals.
