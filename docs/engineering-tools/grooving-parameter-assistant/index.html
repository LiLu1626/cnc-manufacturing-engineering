Grooving fundamentals

RPM = 1000 × Vc ÷ (π × D) — use the workpiece diameter at the cutting point.

Feed rate = f × RPM — grooving feeds are typically 0.05–0.15 mm/rev, much lower than turning because the chip is constrained in the groove.

Groove depth limits: for external grooving, depth should not exceed 1.5× the insert width for stable tool life. Deeper grooves require multiple passes (plunge, retract, shift, plunge again).

Chip control is everything. Grooving produces a wide, thin chip that tends to pack in the groove. Use peck cycles (Q) to retract and break the chip. For stainless and titanium, use 0.5–1 mm pecks.

Example: Grooving a 3 mm wide insert into 50 mm diameter steel at Vc = 120 m/min, f = 0.08 mm/rev. RPM ≈ 764, feed rate ≈ 61 mm/min. At 4 mm depth, depth/width = 1.33 — within single-pass capability. If depth were 8 mm (ratio 2.7), you would need 2–3 plunge cycles with 0.5 mm pecks.
For beginners: Always start at 50% of calculated feed. Watch the chip — it should curl into a small "6" or "9" shape, not a long string. If the chip packs in the groove, reduce feed or increase peck frequency. Never run dry on stainless or titanium.
