Clamping Force Estimator

Estimate clamping force from screw torque or hydraulic cylinder pressure. Knowing your actual clamping force helps prevent part movement, fixture deflection, and part deformation.

Formulas

Screw clamp (approximate): F ≈ (2000 × T) / d × η, where T = torque (N·m), d = bolt diameter (mm), η = friction efficiency factor. This is a shop-floor approximation; actual preload depends on thread condition, washer friction, and bolt grade.

Hydraulic cylinder: F = P × A, where P = pressure (N/mm² = bar × 0.1), A = piston area (mm²).

Rule of thumb: clamping force should be at least 3–5× the maximum cutting force. If the part can flex, spread the force with a larger contact pad. Never clamp on thin walls — support from below.

Example: M10 bolt at 40 N·m torque, oil-lubricated. Approximate clamping force ≈ 12–15 kN. If your cutting force is ~3 kN, that gives a safety factor of 4–5× — adequate. If you torque a dry bolt to the same 40 N·m, friction is higher and actual clamping force drops by ~30%.
