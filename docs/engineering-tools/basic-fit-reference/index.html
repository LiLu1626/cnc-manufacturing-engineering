Understanding ISO fits

Fits are written as hole / shaft, e.g. H7/g6. The capital letter (H) is the hole tolerance; lowercase (g) is the shaft. The number is the tolerance grade (IT5–IT12). Lower grade = tighter tolerance.

  • Hole basis (H): the hole is held to its basic size (H). The shaft letter determines the fit. This is the most common system.
  • Clearance fits: shaft is always smaller than the hole. Gears on shafts, sliding parts, bearings in housings that must slide.
  • Transition fits: may have slight clearance or interference. Precision location, keyed assemblies, pulleys that can be disassembled.
  • Interference fits: shaft is larger than the hole. Requires press, heat, or cold assembly. Permanent or semi-permanent joints, gears on shafts, bearing outer rings.

Exact tolerance values depend on the nominal size (the IT grade band changes by diameter). Always look up the specific numbers in a tolerance table (ISO 286) for your exact size. This page gives the conceptual guide — verify actual limits on the drawing or standard.
