03.4 · Machining Operations

Grinding

Grinding uses an abrasive wheel to remove very small amounts of material. It is used after heat treatment or when you need the highest precision and surface finish — typically Ra < 0.4 µm and tolerances under ±0.005 mm.

Common Grinding Types

TypeWhat it doesTypical use
Surface grindingFlat surfaces with a rotating wheelDie plates, fixture bases, parallel blocks
Cylindrical grindingRotates the part while a wheel grinds ODShafts, pins after heat treat
Internal grindingGrinds precision holesBearing races, dies
Centerless grindingPart rests on a blade, no centersHigh-volume pins, rollers

Why Grind Instead of Mill?

  • After heat treatment, the part is too hard (HRC 50+) for carbide tools.
  • Surface finish requirement is below what turning/milling can hold.
  • Tighter tolerance (±0.002 mm) than machining can achieve.

Key difference from cutting: grinding removes tiny chips (micron-sized) with thousands of abrasive grains. Each grain acts like a tiny cutting tool edge. Material removal rate is low, but accuracy and finish are unmatched.
