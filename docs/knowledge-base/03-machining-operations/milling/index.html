03.2 · Machining Operations

Milling Operations

Milling is the most versatile CNC process: a rotating multi-flute cutter moves across a fixed workpiece. It can machine flat faces, pockets, slots, contours, and 3D shapes. This is what happens in a typical CNC vertical machining center (VMC).

How Milling Works

Common Milling Operations

Face Milling

A large-diameter face mill skims across the top surface, creating a flat plane. Uses indexable inserts at high feed rates. Usually the first operation on a raw block.

Peripheral / Slab Milling

The cutter axis is parallel to the surface, cutting a wide, shallow slab. Used for large flat sides.

Slot Milling

An end mill cuts a full-width slot (ae = tool diameter). Heaviest cut — only half the tool engages, chips are thicker on one side.

Pocket Milling

Milling an enclosed cavity (like a mold pocket). Uses trochoidal or zig-zag toolpaths to manage heat and chip evacuation.

Profile / Contour Milling

The cutter follows the outer perimeter of a part, finishing its side walls. Usually a finishing pass after roughing.

Trochodial Milling

The cutter follows a spiral path with small radial engagement (ae ≈ 10–20% of diameter) but deep axial depth (ap up to 2–3× diameter). Keeps chip thickness constant and tool cool. Great for stainless and titanium.

Climb vs conventional milling: in climb (up) milling, the cutter feeds in the direction of rotation — chip starts thick and gets thin. Better surface finish, less heat, preferred on modern machines. In conventional (down) milling, chip starts thin and gets thick — more rubbing, more heat.
