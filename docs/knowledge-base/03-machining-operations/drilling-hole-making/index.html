03.3 · Machining Operations

Drilling & Hole Making

Holes are the most common machining feature — and the most trouble-prone. A hole drilled in open air is easy; a deep, cross-hole, threaded, or precision hole requires the right sequence of tools and operations.

The Hole-Making Sequence

Most holes are not drilled in one step. A typical precision hole looks like this:

Deep Hole Drilling

When depth exceeds 3× diameter (3D), chips can't escape and the drill burns. Solutions:

  • Peck drilling (G83): retract fully every 1–2× diameter to clear chips.
  • High-pressure coolant: flushes chips out through the drill flutes.
  • Internal coolant drills: drills with holes through the flutes.

Calculate peck depth and feed: Drilling Parameter Assistant

Drill wander: a drill on a flat surface can "walk" sideways if not spotted first. Always spot-drill first, especially on angled or curved surfaces. This is the #1 cause of hole position errors.
