# Engineering Case Studies

A structured record of manufacturing-engineering decisions, technical validation, and transferable process learning.

[← Platform Home](../)

## Purpose

Case studies turn individual projects into reusable engineering knowledge. They document not only what was changed, but why a decision was made, the constraints considered, how the result was verified, and where the approach may or may not be reused.

## Case Study Standard

Each case study should contain:

1. **Engineering Context** — Part family, material, production environment, machine platform, and relevant quality or delivery requirement.
2. **Problem Definition** — Observable condition, affected characteristic, baseline performance, and business or technical impact.
3. **Constraints and Risks** — Drawing requirements, datum structure, workholding, tooling, equipment limits, safety, material condition, and validation constraints.
4. **Root-Cause Investigation** — Evidence, measurement method, hypotheses, and eliminated causes.
5. **Engineering Options** — Alternatives evaluated, expected tradeoffs, and criteria for selection.
6. **Implemented Countermeasure** — Process, program, fixture, tooling, inspection, or workflow change introduced.
7. **Validation** — First-off checks, dimensional or capability data, cycle-time evidence, tool-life data, repeatability assessment, and production release criteria.
8. **Result and Transferability** — Quantified outcome where appropriate, limitations, and conditions for reuse.

## Case Categories

### Process Development

Development of a stable manufacturing route for a new part, material, tolerance requirement, or production volume.

### Quality and Capability Improvement

Investigation and improvement of dimensional variation, surface condition, burr formation, repeatability, or inspection effectiveness.

### Productivity and Cost Improvement

Cycle-time reduction, tool-life improvement, changeover reduction, automation integration, or material-removal optimization without compromising quality.

### Reliability and Risk Reduction

Improvements addressing collision risk, setup error, workholding stability, machine reliability, chip control, safety, or process recovery.

## Evidence Before Narrative

A useful case study is based on measured evidence rather than conclusions alone. Screenshots, inspection data, setup documentation, process sheets, tool records, and approved program revisions can be referenced when sharing them does not expose confidential customer or employer information.

## Confidentiality and Professional Ethics

Customer identifiers, controlled drawings, commercial pricing, proprietary process details, and employer-confidential information must not be published. Cases can still teach valuable methods by anonymizing parts, removing sensitive values, and focusing on the engineering reasoning and verification method.
