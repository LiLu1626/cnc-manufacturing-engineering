Case: Turning Wear Diagnosis

A lathe insert fails prematurely. Following the method from page 27, we read the edge, name the wear type, isolate the cause, change one variable, and verify — instead of just swapping inserts.

Level: KPrerequisite: pages 25–28Scope: worked case
After this lesson, you can:
  • Apply the diagnosis method to a real turning failure
  • Distinguish wear, BUE and chipping in the evidence
  • Record the corrective action and verification

Concept

This case walks the page-27 loop: observe the worn insert, separate gradual wear from premature failure, match morphology to cause, change one variable, verify. The lathe operation is OD turning of a stainless bar.

Why It Matters

Premature insert failure in production costs money per stop. A structured diagnosis turns a recurring nuisance into a fixed parameter or grade, and the record feeds tool management (page 30).

How It Works

Observe: the insert shows built-up edge on the nose and a poor finish, not a smooth wear land. Separate: premature — the edge is rubbing and welding material. Hypothesize: speed too low for the work-hardening stainless. Change one thing: raise cutting speed within grade limits (and confirm chipformer). Verify: BUE reduces and finish improves; tool life logged on the trial sheet.

Worked Example

The same method would branch differently by evidence: a crater on the rake points to hot/crater wear (adjust grade or speed), chipping points to shock (tougher grade/rigidity). Here the welded nose says low speed, not overload. Each branch changes a different lever.

Classroom assumption: the stainless and BUE scenario is illustrative; confirm the speed adjustment within the insert catalog's range for the grade.

Reading the record

The worn insert from the stainless bar shows a built-up nose and a torn-looking finish, while the flank land itself is modest — so this is not an insert that wore out from long service. The diagnosis (page 27) points to low speed in a work-hardening material, not to a weak grade. The single change is raising vc within the catalog's stainless range and confirming the chipformer is suited to gummy flow. On the follow-up run, BUE should shrink and the finish improve; if instead crater or flank wear accelerates, the speed was pushed too far and the grade should step up. The result goes on the wear-trial log so the next stainless job starts at the corrected speed, not at the old guess.

Common Mistakes

  • Reaching for a tougher grade when BUE is the issue. BUE is speed-related. Fix: read the morphology first.
  • Changing speed and feed at once. Fix: one variable, then verify.

Practice

Q1. Welded material on the nose suggests what?

Built-up edge, usually too-low speed in a gummy/work-hardening material.

Q2. What is the single first change?

Raise speed within grade limits, then re-observe BUE and finish.
