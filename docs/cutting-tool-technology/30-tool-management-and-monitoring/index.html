Tool Management & Monitoring

A shop that loses track of which tool has how many parts on it wastes time and scrap. Tool identification, offset records and simple life tracking are the basics; monitoring catches wear before it ruins a part.

Level: BPrerequisite: 28 Tool Life TestingScope: shop practice
After this lesson, you can:
  • Identify tools and record offsets correctly
  • Track tool life by parts or time
  • Describe simple in-process wear monitoring

Concept

Tool management means knowing which tool is where, its offset values, and how many parts it has cut. Life tracking counts parts or time per edge so the tool is replaced before it fails. Monitoring — from operator observation to load/acoustic sensors — flags wear or breakage during the run.

Why It Matters

Without tracking, a tool runs until it scrapes a part or breaks in the cut. With simple records, you replace the edge on schedule, reuse known offsets, and reorder before stock runs out. Management turns tooling from a fire-fight into a controlled process.

How It Works

Label each tool assembly, record its length/diameter offset, and log parts per edge. When the count reaches the proven life limit, index or replace. Light monitoring (spindle load rising, finish changing) gives early warning between scheduled changes.

Worked Example

A recurring job uses T03; the proven life is 120 parts. The operator logs each run and indexes T03 at 120 parts — before wear hurts the bore. Offsets are recorded so a replacement tool touches in once and repeats. No surprise scrapped bores.

Common Mistakes

  • Running a tool past its proven life. It scrapes. Fix: count parts and change on schedule.
  • Losing offset records. Setup time grows. Fix: log tool offsets with the assembly.

Practice

Q1. Why count parts per edge?

To replace the tool on schedule before wear or breakage ruins parts.
