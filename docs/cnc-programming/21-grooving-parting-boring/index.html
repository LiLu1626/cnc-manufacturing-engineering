Grooving, Parting & Boring

Grooving, parting, and boring use special tools and toolpaths. This page covers groove programming, cutoff operations, and internal boring.

Concept

Grooving uses a narrow tool to cut a recess. Parting (cutoff) separates the finished part from the bar. Boring uses an internal tool to enlarge an existing hole. Each has unique tool geometry and feed/speed considerations.

Why It Matters

Groove tools are weak in X and prone to chatter. Parting tools must clear the chuck. Boring bars deflect at length, limiting depth. Wrong feeds cause chatter, poor finish, or tool breakage.

How It Works

Grooving

Groove tool moves in X to the groove depth, then may move in Z to widen. Program X depth as diameter. For a 2 mm wide groove at O30: feed X to O26, dwell, retract to O30.

Parting

Part off at the finished length. Feed X to near center (leave ~0.5 mm), then stop and break off, or feed through at very slow feed. Never feed through at normal feed — it is dangerous.

Boring

Boring bar reaches into a drilled hole. X moves from smaller bore to larger (opposite of OD turning). Depth limited by bar length-to-diameter ratio (keep < 4:1 for steel).

Example

Part off at Z-45 from O40 bar: position groove tool at X42 Z-45, feed X to X1 at F0.05 mm/rev. At S800, feed = 40 mm/min. Radial cut depth = (40-1)/2 = 19.5 mm. Time = 19.5/40 ≈ 29 s.

Common Mistakes

  • Normal feed on parting — parting feed should be 0.05-0.1 mm/rev, much slower than turning.
  • Boring bar too long — length-to-diameter > 4:1 causes chatter and deflection.
  • Forgetting dwell in groove — dwell at bottom cleans up the groove floor.

Practice

1. Groove to O26 from O35. Radial depth?

Ans

(35-26)/2 = 4.5 mm.

2. Why slow feed on parting?

Ans

The tool cuts at increasing diameter-to-center ratio; slow feed prevents chatter and tool breakage as the cross-section thins.

3. Boring direction: X moves from smaller to larger or larger to smaller?

Ans

Smaller to larger (enlarging the bore).
