Grooving, Parting & Boring

Grooving, parting (cutoff), and boring use special tools and toolpaths that differ from OD turning. This page covers groove programming, cutoff operations, internal boring, and the feed/speed considerations that prevent chatter and tool breakage.

Concept

Grooving uses a narrow, rectangular insert to cut a recess (groove) into the part. The tool feeds radially in X to the groove depth, may dwell to clean the floor, and retracts. Parting (cutoff) is a specialized groove operation: the tool feeds radially all the way to near center to separate the finished part from the bar stock. Boring uses an internal boring bar to enlarge an existing drilled hole from the inside. Each operation has unique tool geometry, feed rates, and rigidity challenges.

Why It Matters

Groove tools are narrow and weak in the X direction — they chatter easily and break if fed too fast. Parting tools must clear the chuck and the finished part. Boring bars deflect because they reach into a hole, limiting how deep you can bore. Wrong feeds cause chatter, poor surface finish, or snapped tools. These operations demand slower feeds than OD turning and careful toolpath planning.

How It Works

Grooving

The groove tool is positioned at the correct Z location, then feeds radially in X to the groove diameter. For a 2 mm wide groove at Ø30 on a Ø35 bar: position the tool at X37 Z−20, feed X to X30, dwell briefly, then retract to X37. X is programmed as diameter, so the radial depth is (35−30)/2 = 2.5 mm.

For wider grooves, the tool may take multiple Z passes: cut to depth, shift Z by the tool width minus overlap, cut again, until the groove is the desired width. A dwell at the bottom cleans up the groove floor and reduces chatter.

Parting (Cutoff)

Part off at the finished length: position the groove/parting tool at the Z face, then feed radially toward center at very slow feed (0.05–0.1 mm/rev). As the tool approaches center, the remaining cross-section gets thinner. Never feed through to X0 at normal feed — the tool grabs and snaps. Instead, feed to about X1 (leaving a thin nub), stop the spindle, and break the part off by hand, or feed through at very slow feed under constant supervision.

Boring

A boring bar reaches into a pre-drilled hole. The X direction is opposite to OD turning: X starts at the drilled hole diameter and increases as the bore gets larger (you are cutting from the inside out). Boring bar rigidity is limited by length-to-diameter ratio: keep the bar overhang under 4× the bar diameter for steel, or deflection and chatter ruin the bore finish.

Example

Parting example: Part off at Z−45 from a Ø40 bar. S800 RPM, parting feed F0.05 mm/rev. Position the parting tool at X42 Z−45. Feed X toward center.

T0505                    (parting tool)
S800 M03
G00 X42. Z-45.
G01 X1.0 F0.05          (feed to near center, 0.05 mm/rev)
G00 X42.                 (retract)
M05

Feed rate calculation: At S800 and F0.05 mm/rev, the feed in mm/min is 800 × 0.05 = 40 mm/min. The radial cut from X42 to X1 is (42−1)/2 = 20.5 mm. Time = 20.5/40 = 0.51 minutes = 31 seconds. Note the slow feed: OD turning at F0.2 would snap the parting tool.

Common Mistakes

  • Normal feed on parting — parting feed must be 0.05–0.1 mm/rev, much slower than turning. Normal feed snaps the tool.
  • Boring bar too long — length-to-diameter ratio over 4:1 causes chatter and deflection. Use the shortest bar possible.
  • No dwell at groove bottom — a brief dwell cleans up the groove floor and reduces tool marks.
  • Feeding to X0 on parting — leaving a thin nub and breaking by hand is safer than feeding through at speed.
  • Boring X direction reversed — X increases as the bore gets larger, opposite to OD turning.

Practice

1. Groove to Ø26 from Ø35 stock. What is the radial depth?

Ans

(35 − 26) / 2 = 4.5 mm radial depth.

2. Why use slow feed on parting?

Ans

As the tool approaches center, the remaining cross-section thins rapidly. Slow feed prevents the tool from grabbing and breaking, and produces a cleaner break.

3. In boring, does X move from smaller to larger or larger to smaller?

Ans

From smaller to larger — you are enlarging the bore from the inside. X increases as the bore diameter grows.

4. A boring bar is 20 mm diameter and 120 mm overhang. Is this OK?

Ans

No. Length-to-diameter ratio = 120/20 = 6:1, well over the 4:1 limit. Expect chatter and deflection. Use a shorter bar or larger diameter bar.
