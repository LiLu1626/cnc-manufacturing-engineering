Grooving, Parting, and Boring

These operations use specialized tools: grooving blades for O-ring and retaining ring grooves, cutoff blades for parting off, and boring bars for internal turning.

What you will learn:
  • Groove tool geometry and infeed direction
  • Parting off: feed, speed, and safety
  • Boring bars: rigidity, overhang, and deflection
  • Programming differences from OD turning

Grooving

A groove tool feeds radially (X direction) into the part to cut a recess. The tool width determines the groove width; multiple X passes may be needed for wide grooves. Program at the groove bottom diameter.

Parting Off

A cutoff (parting) tool feeds radially until the part separates from the bar. Use reduced feed for the last 1-2 mm to prevent a burr. Always make sure there is clearance behind the part for the blade to exit.

Boring

A boring bar reaches into a pre-drilled hole and machines the ID. Boring bars are inherently less rigid than OD tools — keep overhang minimal and use light cuts to avoid chatter.

Common Mistakes

  • Parting off without enough clearance behind the part — the blade crashes into the chuck.
  • Using too long a boring bar — chatter and poor surface finish.
  • Forgetting that groove tool nose radius affects the bottom corner.

Practice

1. Which direction does a groove tool primarily feed?

Show answer

Radially (X direction on a lathe). It feeds in to the groove depth, then may move along Z for face grooving.

2. Why should boring bar overhang be minimized?

Show answer

Boring bars deflect easily. Longer overhang increases chatter, poor finish, and dimensional error.
