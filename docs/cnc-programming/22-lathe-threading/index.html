Lathe Threading Cycles

On a Haas lathe, G76 cuts a thread automatically. K is the total thread height, D is the first-pass depth, and F is the lead. They are not interchangeable.

Level: KeyMachine: Lathe
What you will learn:
  • Haas G76 one-line format and each parameter
  • K = total thread height (radial depth from major to minor)
  • D = first cut depth (depth of the initial pass)
  • F = lead (mm/rev); G97 constant RPM required

Concept

Single-point threading uses a form tool that follows the thread helix. The G76 cycle automates multiple passes with decreasing infeed. On a Haas lathe, G76 is programmed on one line with separate parameters for the total depth and the first cut.

Why It Matters

K and D are not synonyms. K defines how deep the thread ends up; D defines how aggressive the very first pass bites. Confusing them — or omitting D — leads to broken tools or wrong thread depth. Also, FANUC's two-line G76 (P/Q/R) is different; copying it onto a Haas causes alarms.

Haas G76 Parameters

G76 X_ Z_ K_ D_ F_
  • X — final (minor) diameter of the thread at the end.
  • Z — end position of the thread along Z.
  • K — total thread height, radial depth from the major diameter to the minor diameter (always positive).
  • D — depth of the first cut (smaller than K; the cycle distributes remaining passes automatically).
  • F — lead in mm/rev (for single-start threads, F = pitch).

Worked Example: M10×1.5 External

Major diameter = 10 mm. Pitch = 1.5 mm. For a 60° metric thread, the theoretical thread height (radial) = 0.613 × pitch = 0.613 × 1.5 ≈ 0.92 mm. This is a geometric ideal for a sharp 60° V-thread; actual minor diameter depends on tolerance class (6g/6H), tool nose radius, and chamfer. Always verify against the thread standard.

Minor diameter = major − 2 × K = 10 − 2 × 0.92 = 8.16 mm (theoretical; adjust per tolerance).

First cut depth D = 0.2 mm is a teaching starting point for carbide inserts in steel. Real values depend on insert grade, material, coolant, and machine rigidity — consult the tool manufacturer's recommendations.

G97 S500 M03            (constant RPM — required for threading)
G00 X11. Z3.            (approach: above stock, 3mm lead-in)
G76 X8.16 Z-20. K0.92 D0.2 F1.5

Reading the line: final minor diameter X8.16, thread ends at Z−20, total radial thread height K0.92, first pass D0.2, lead F1.5 mm/rev. The controller takes successive passes with decreasing depth until reaching X8.16.

Why G97, Not G96

Threading requires the spindle to turn at a constant RPM so the lead (mm/rev) stays consistent. G96 (constant surface speed) would increase RPM as the tool cuts deeper and diameter shrinks, distorting the pitch. Always use G97 S_ M03 before G76.

Common Mistakes

  • Treating K and D as interchangeable — K is total depth, D is first-pass depth; both are required.
  • Computing X as major − K instead of major − 2×K — the depth is radial, so diameter reduces by twice K.
  • Forgetting G97 — G96 varies RPM and ruins pitch.
  • Using FANUC two-line G76 (P/Q format) on a Haas — syntax mismatch causes alarms.
  • Not leaving lead-in distance (Z3. before the thread start) — the spindle needs time to sync.

Practice

1. M10×1.5: what is K, and what is the minor diameter?

Show answer

K ≈ 0.613 × 1.5 = 0.92 mm. Minor diameter = 10 − 2×0.92 = 8.16 mm.

2. In G76 X8.16 Z-20 K0.92 D0.2 F1.5, what does D0.2 mean?

Show answer

The first pass cuts 0.2 mm deeper radially than the starting diameter. Subsequent passes are distributed by the cycle until total K0.92 is reached.

3. Why must G97 (not G96) be active during threading?

Show answer

Threading needs constant RPM so F (mm/rev = lead) produces the correct pitch. G96 would change RPM as diameter changes, ruining thread form.
