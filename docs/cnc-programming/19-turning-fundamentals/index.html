Turning Coordinates & Toolpaths

CNC lathes differ from mills in axes and programming. This page covers the lathe coordinate system, diameter programming, and basic turning operations.

Concept

A CNC lathe has two axes: X (radial, programmed in diameter) and Z (axial, along the spindle). The workpiece rotates; the tool moves. X40 means 40 mm diameter, not 40 mm radial travel.

Z0 is typically on the front face of the part. Positive Z moves away from the chuck; negative Z moves toward the chuck.

Why It Matters

Programming X as radius on a diameter-mode lathe cuts diameters 50% too large or too small. The most common turning mistake. Also, Z direction: cutting into the part is negative Z, not positive.

How It Works

Diameter Programming

X is always diameter. Tool at X30 = 30 mm diameter. Movement from X40 to X30 = 5 mm radial cut (10 mm diameter reduction).

Basic Operations

  • Facing — tool moves X from OD to center at Z0
  • OD turning — tool moves Z along the part at fixed X
  • ID boring — tool moves inside a bore at fixed X

Turret

Tools are indexed by T code (T0101 = tool 1, offset 1). No M06 needed — the turret indexes directly.

Example

Turn from O40 to O30 over Z50: starting at X42 Z2, feed to X30 Z0, then Z-50. At F0.2 mm/rev and S1000, feed = 200 mm/min. Ideal time for 50 mm Z cut = 50/200 = 15 s.

Common Mistakes

  • Treating X as radius — X is diameter; radial movement is half the X difference.
  • Z direction reversed — cutting into the part is negative Z.
  • Forgetting T0101 offset — T01 selects tool; T0101 activates both tool and offset.

Practice

1. X moves from O50 to O44. Radial depth?

Ans

(50-44)/2 = 3 mm.

2. Z0 is where?

Ans

On the front finished face of the part.

3. Why no M06 on a lathe?

Ans

The turret indexes tools directly. T0202 indexes to tool 2 and activates its offset.
