Turning Fundamentals

A CNC lathe rotates the workpiece and tools move in X (diameter) and Z (along the part). Understanding diameter programming is the first step.

What you will learn:
  • Lathe axes: X (diameter), Z (along spindle)
  • Diameter programming vs radius programming
  • Spindle direction and turret orientation
  • Basic turning operations: face, OD, bore

Lathe Coordinates

On a CNC lathe, Z runs along the spindle axis (toward/away from the chuck). X is the radial axis. Most lathes use diameter programming: X values are the actual diameter of the part, not the radius. A 40 mm bar has X40.0, not X20.0.

Diameter vs Radius

Diameter programming means the X coordinate you read on the drawing (the OD) goes directly into the program. If you see a 50 mm diameter on the drawing, you write X50.0. The controller knows that radius = X/2.

Spindle and Turret

On most horizontal lathes, the spindle turns clockwise (M03) when viewed from the headstock. The turret holds multiple tools. Positive X moves away from the centerline (larger diameter); positive Z moves away from the chuck.

Basic Operations

  • Facing: tool moves across the end of the part to square it.
  • OD turning: tool moves along Z to reduce the diameter.
  • Boring: tool enters a hole and moves along Z to enlarge it internally.

Common Mistakes

  • Programming X as radius instead of diameter — the cut is half as large as intended.
  • Confusing positive/negative Z direction relative to the chuck.
  • Forgetting that lathe feed is in mm/rev (G99 on Haas), not mm/min.

Practice

1. A drawing shows an OD of 35 mm. What X value do you program?

Show answer

X35.0 (diameter programming). Not X17.5.

2. On a lathe, which axis runs along the spindle?

Show answer

Z. X is the radial/diameter axis.
