Rapid & Linear Motion

G00 and G01 are the workhorses of CNC motion — rapid positioning and linear feed. This page explains when to use which, why G00 is not straight-line, safe approach and retract patterns, and how to estimate cutting time.

Concept

G00 (rapid positioning) moves the tool at the machine's maximum rapid traverse rate (typically 15–40 m/min) for non-cutting moves. G01 (linear interpolation) moves the tool at the programmed feed rate F while cutting in a straight line. Both are modal: once commanded, they stay active until another motion G code replaces them.

A critical difference: G01 always interpolates a straight line between the start and end point — all axes move together at coordinated feed. G00 may move axes independently: on many machines, X reaches its end before Y, so the path is two segments (an L-shape), not a diagonal. This means G00 can hit obstacles that a straight diagonal would clear, and vice versa.

Why It Matters

The #1 crash cause on CNC mills is G00 XY motion while the tool is at cut depth. A safe program always: positions XY first at a safe Z, then lowers Z to cut depth, does the XY cutting, then raises Z before the next XY move. Breaking this sequence — moving sideways while the tool is buried in the part — gouges the part, snaps the tool, or crashes into the fixture.

How It Works

The Safe Approach Pattern

  1. G00 to XY target — position above the cut at a safe Z height.
  2. G00 Z to clearance plane — rapid down to R2 or R5 above the stock.
  3. G01 Z to cut depth — feed down to Z depth at a controlled feed rate.
  4. G01 XY cut moves — feed along the cut path at cutting feed.
  5. G00 Z retract — rapid back up to clearance plane.
  6. G00 to next XY — rapid to the next position at safe Z.

This XY-first, Z-second sequence is the foundation of safe milling. Never move XY while Z is at cut depth.

G00 Is Not Always a Straight Line

On many CNC machines, G00 commands each axis to move at its own maximum speed. If X and Y have different rapid rates, or if the distances are different, one axis arrives first. The tool path is not a clean diagonal — it is an L-shape or dog-leg. This matters for collision checking: visualize both axes independently, not as a straight diagonal line.

Cutting Time Calculation

Steady-state cutting time = distance ÷ feed rate. For a 50 mm cut at F300 mm/min: 50/300 = 0.167 min = 10 seconds. For short moves (under 10 mm), add acceleration/deceleration time — the tool never reaches programmed feed. For rough estimates, the distance/feed formula is accurate enough.

Example

G00 X0 Y0        (rapid XY above first position, at Z50)
G00 Z5.          (rapid to 5 mm clearance)
G01 Z-5. F100.   (feed down to Z-5 at 100 mm/min)
G01 X50. F300.   (cut 50 mm at 300 mm/min = 10 s)
G00 Z5.          (retract to clearance BEFORE XY)
G00 X100. Y0.    (rapid to next position at safe Z)
G01 Z-5. F100.   (feed down again)
G01 X150. F300.  (next cut)
G00 Z50.         (final retract)

Trace: Note the order at every step: Z goes up before XY moves. The cut at Z−5 is always followed by G00 Z5. before any G00 X move. This is the safe pattern.

Common Mistakes

  • G00 XY at cut depth — always retract Z to clearance before moving XY. This is the #1 crash cause.
  • Forgetting F on the first G01 — the first G01 after G00 needs an F command. Without F, the machine may use a default or alarm.
  • Assuming G00 is straight-line — axes move independently; visualize the dog-leg path for collision checking.
  • Feeding Z at cutting feed too late — the first G01 Z needs a plunge feed (slower than XY feed) to push chips out.
  • No clearance plane between cuts — always retract to Z5 or higher between features.

Practice

1. Cut length 75 mm at F250. Cutting time?

Ans

75 / 250 = 0.3 minutes = 18 seconds.

2. Why retract Z before moving XY?

Ans

At cut depth, rapid XY would drag the tool through the part or fixture. Retracting Z to clearance first ensures the tool moves above the work.

3. What is the path difference between G00 and G01?

Ans

G01 interpolates a coordinated straight line at feed. G00 moves each axis at max speed independently — the path may be an L-shape, not a diagonal.

4. Why does the first G01 Z use a slower feed than XY?

Ans

Plunging straight down into material is harder than cutting sideways. A slower plunge feed (100 mm/min vs 300 mm/min) pushes chips out and prevents tool jam.
