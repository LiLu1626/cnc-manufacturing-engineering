Drilling and Hole-Making Cycles

Canned drilling cycles (G81, G83, G84) automate positioning, plunge, feed, and retract. Each has a defined R plane and depth.

What you will learn:
  • G81 simple drill, G83 peck drill, G84 tapping
  • R plane, initial plane, Z depth
  • G80 cancels the cycle
  • On Haas: XY movement continues the cycle; explicit G00 cancels it

Cycle Structure

A drilling cycle combines four moves into one line: rapid to R plane, feed to Z depth, retract to R plane (or initial plane on G80), then rapid to next position.

Common Cycles

  • G81: simple drill — rapid to R, feed to Z, retract to R plane.
  • G83: peck drill — feeds in increments, retracts to break chips (deep holes).
  • G84: tapping — feed down and reverse tap out at synchronized speed.
  • G80: cancel cycle — retracts to initial plane.

Cycle Cancellation on Haas

Once G81 is active, writing a new X/Y position automatically repeats the drill cycle at that position. To stop cycling, write G80 or use an explicit G00 — on Haas, G00 cancels the canned cycle mode. Do not rely on the next rapid to "just move" — it will drill.

Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting G80 before moving at Z height — the next XY move drills a hole.
  • Using G81 for deep holes — chips pack and the tool binds; use G83 peck.
  • Setting the R plane too low — rapid approach collides with fixtures.

Practice

1. After G81 Z-10 R2, you write X50. What happens?

Show answer

The machine moves to X50 and drills a hole to Z-10 — the cycle is modal. You must write G80 to cancel.

2. When do you use G83 instead of G81?

Show answer

For deep holes where chips need to be broken and cleared. G83 pecks back to the R plane between cuts.
