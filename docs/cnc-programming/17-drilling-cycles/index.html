Drilling, Pecking & Boring

Drilling cycles automate repeated hole-making. This page covers G81, G82, G83, G85 — when to use each, R plane, depth, and retract modes.

Concept

A drilling cycle is a canned sequence: rapid to R plane, feed to Z depth, retract. G81 is simple drilling. G82 dwells at the bottom (for spot faces). G83 pecks in increments (for deep holes). G85 bores with feed in and feed out.

Why It Matters

Deep holes without pecking pack chips and break the drill. Wrong R plane means the tool retracts into chips or collides with the fixture. G98 vs G99 retract height determines whether the tool clears the workpiece between holes.

How It Works

Cycle Parameters

  • X/Y — hole position
  • R — R plane (start of feed, above stock)
  • Z — final depth
  • F — feed rate
  • G98 — retract to initial Z plane
  • G99 — retract to R plane

Common Cycles

  • G81 — simple drill, no dwell
  • G82 — drill with dwell at bottom
  • G83 — peck drill (chip breaking)
  • G85 — bore, feed in/feed out

Example

G90 G54 G00 X10. Y10.
S1500 M03
G43 H01 Z50. M08
G98 G81 R2. Z-15. F120.
X50. Y30.          (second hole, same cycle)
G80 G00 Z50. M09

G98 retracts to Z50 between holes (clear of workpiece). G99 would retract to R2. For plate drilling with no obstructions, G99 saves time; for holes around a boss, G98 clears it.

Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting G80 — cycle stays active on next X/Y move.
  • Ignoring drill point geometry — the drill tip adds ~0.3×D extra depth. For a 10 mm drill, add 3 mm breakthrough.
  • G98 vs G99 wrong choice — G99 around a fixture causes collision.

Practice

1. Through-hole in 12 mm plate, O10 drill. Programmed Z depth?

Ans

12 + 3 (tip) = 15 mm. Z-15. from top surface.

2. When use G83 over G81?

Ans

Deep holes (>3×D) where chips pack. G83 pecks to break and clear chips.

3. What does G98 do?

Ans

Retract to initial Z plane after drilling. G99 retracts only to R plane.
