Multi-Axis and Probing Overview

Beyond 3-axis: rotary axes (A/B/C), 4/5-axis machining, and touch probes that measure the part while it is still on the machine.

What you will learn:
  • Rotary axes A, B, C and their relationships to X/Y/Z
  • 4-axis indexing vs full 5-axis simultaneous
  • Touch probes: G31 records contact position
  • Probing does not automatically update offsets

Rotary Axes

A rotates around X, B around Y, C around Z. A 4-axis mill adds a rotary table (usually A or C). A 5-axis machine has two rotary axes for simultaneous orientation.

4-Axis vs 5-Axis

4-axis indexing: the rotary axis positions the part, then milling happens in 3 axes while it stays still. 5-axis simultaneous: the tool moves and rotates continuously during the cut — complex programming, usually CAM-generated.

Touch Probing

A probe in the spindle touches the part and records its position via G31. This measures edges, centerlines, and bores. Important: G31 records the position to a variable; it does not automatically update G54. You calculate the edge or center and enter the offset. See Probing Basics for the edge-finding calculation.

Common Mistakes

  • Thinking G31 automatically sets work zero — it records; you calculate.
  • Probing at high feed — the probe is delicate.
  • Forgetting probe tip radius in the calculation.

Practice

1. Which rotary axis rotates around Z?

Show answer

C axis. A rotates around X, B around Y, C around Z.

2. Does G31 update G54 automatically?

Show answer

No. It records the touch position to a variable. You calculate and enter the offset yourself.
