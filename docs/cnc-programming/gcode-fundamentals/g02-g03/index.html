02.2 · G02 / G03

G02 / G03 — Circular Interpolation

To cut arcs and circles, the machine moves two axes simultaneously at varying speeds. G02 is clockwise, G03 is counterclockwise. The trick is telling the machine where the arc center is.

How to Specify an Arc

You need three things: start point (where you are), end point (X/Y destination), and arc center (where the circle's center is relative to the start).

Two Ways to Define the Center

MethodSyntaxHow it works
I / JG02 X__ Y__ I__ J__I = center offset in X from start, J = center offset in Y from start
Radius RG02 X__ Y__ R__Just give the radius. Simpler, but R can't distinguish full circles.

Example: Cut a quarter circle

Start at X0 Y0, end at X20 Y20, center at X0 Y20 (radius 20). Clockwise (G02):

G01 X0 Y0 F300.
G02 X20. Y20. I0 J20.

Or using R: G02 X20. Y20. R20.

G02 vs G03 Direction

Look at the arc from the positive Z direction (down at the table on a mill). Clockwise = G02. Counterclockwise = G03. On lathes, the convention is reversed because of how you view the part — check your controller.

Common mistake: I/J are incremental from the start point, not absolute coordinates. If the start is at X5 Y5 and center is at X10 Y5, then I=5, J=0 — not I=10. This trips up beginners constantly. When in doubt, use R.
