Milling Strategies

How you move the tool matters as much as where you move it. Climb vs conventional, stepover, radial engagement, and chip thinning all affect tool life and finish.

What you will learn:
  • Climb (down) milling vs conventional (up) milling
  • Radial engagement (ae) and axial depth (ap)
  • Stepover for face milling and pocketing
  • Why chip thinning matters at low radial engagement

Climb vs Conventional

Climb milling (down cut): the tool feeds in the same direction as the rotation at the cut point. Chip thickness starts thick and tapers — better finish, less wear, preferred on modern machines.

Conventional milling (up cut): the tool feeds against the rotation. Chip starts thin and gets thick — rubbing work-hardens the surface, more tool wear. Used on older machines with backlash.

Engagement

Radial engagement (ae) is how much of the cutter diameter is in the cut (stepover). Axial depth (ap) is how deep the cut is axially. For finishing pockets, ae is small and ap may be full flute length.

Common Mistakes

  • Full slotting (ae = 100% of diameter) with an end mill — too much heat and chip packing.
  • Not considering chip thinning at low ae — feed per tooth must increase to maintain chip thickness.
  • Plunging straight down with a flat-end mill unless it has center-cutting geometry.

Practice

1. Which milling direction is preferred on a modern CNC mill?

Show answer

Climb (down) milling. Chip starts thick, less rubbing, better finish, and modern machines have minimal backlash.

2. Why can you not plunge with a regular end mill?

Show answer

Regular end mills have no center cutting edge. Plunge straight in and the rubs at the center instead of cutting. Use a center-cutting end mill, a spot drill, or ramp in.
