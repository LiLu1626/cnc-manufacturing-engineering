Milling Strategies: Facing, Slots & Pockets

Milling has distinct strategies for different features. This page covers facing, slots, pockets, and the climb vs conventional cut decision.

Concept

Face milling removes material from the top surface. Slot milling cuts a channel using full-width engagement. Pocket milling clears an enclosed area. Each strategy uses different stepover, depth, and toolpath.

Why It Matters

Using a 10 mm end mill to slot through a 12 mm deep block in one pass overloads the spindle. Pocketing without climb milling causes chatter. Strategy choice directly affects tool life, surface finish, and cycle time.

How It Works

Stepover (Radial Engagement)

For finishing: stepover = 25-40% of cutter diameter. For roughing: up to 50-75%. A 10 mm tool at 50% stepover takes 5 mm wide passes.

Climb vs Conventional

  • Climb (up-milling) — tool rotates same direction as feed. Chip starts thick, ends thin. Better finish, preferred on CNC machines.
  • Conventional (down-milling) — tool rotates opposite feed. Chip starts thin, rubs. Causes work hardening.

Example

Face mill a 100 mm wide plate with O50 face mill: stepover = 35 mm (70% of O50). Number of passes = 100/35 = 3 passes. At F300, each pass traverses 100 mm. Total facing time ≈ 3 × 100/300 = 60 seconds.

Common Mistakes

  • Full-width slotting in one deep pass — use peck cycles or smaller depth per pass.
  • Conventional milling on CNC — CNC machines prefer climb; conventional causes chatter and tool wear.
  • Stepover too wide — exceeds spindle power and torque.

Practice

1. O12 end mill, 50% stepover. Width per pass?

Ans

6 mm.

2. Climb or conventional on CNC?

Ans

Climb. CNC machines have no backlash to worry about.

3. Pocket 40×40, O10 tool. How many raster passes?

Ans

40/(10×0.5) = 8 passes (at 50% stepover).
