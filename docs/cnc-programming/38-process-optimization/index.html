Process Optimization

Once a basic program runs safely, the next step is making it faster, more reliable, and gentler on tooling. This page covers the practical levers a programmer can pull without rewriting the whole process.

Cycle Time Reduction

The biggest gains usually come from cutting air time and reducing non-cut moves, not from cranking feed and speed.

  • Rapid optimization: Stack Z retracts only as high as needed. Use G00 multi-axis moves when clearance allows.
  • Tool change consolidation: Combine operations under one tool where geometry permits. Each tool change costs 5–15 seconds.
  • Feed and speed: Increase feed per tooth (chip load) before increasing RPM. A heavier chip at lower RPM often improves tool life and surface finish.
  • Cutting strategy: Trochoidal milling for deep slots reduces radial engagement and heat. Climb milling (vs conventional) improves tool life and surface.

Tool Life and Wear

Every tool wears. The goal is predictable, gradual wear rather than catastrophic failure.

Surface Finish

Theoretical feed marks come from the tool nose radius sweeping across the part. The peak-to-valley height (Rmax) of the ideal feed mark is:

Rmax ≈ f² / (8 × r) where f = feed mm/rev, r = nose radius mm.

Worked example: f = 0.2 mm/rev, r = 0.4 mm.
Rmax = 0.2² / (8 × 0.4) = 0.04 / 3.2 = 0.0125 mm = 12.5 µm.

This is the theoretical peak-to-valley height of the feed mark, not the arithmetic average Ra. Actual surface texture also depends on tool wear, built-up edge, machine vibration, and material — the formula is a geometric estimate only, not a guaranteed finish. Always measure with a profilometer.

Back-calculation: for Rmax = 1.6 µm = 0.0016 mm with r = 0.4 mm, solve f: f = √(8 × r × Rmax) = √(8 × 0.4 × 0.0016) = √0.00512 ≈ 0.072 mm/rev.

Stability and Chatter

Chatter (vibration marks) is the #1 enemy of finish and tool life. The fix hierarchy:

  1. Reduce radial width of cut (ae) — often the biggest single lever.
  2. Increase feed per tooth to move the cutting force frequency.
  3. Shorten tool overhang. Every doubling of overhang drops rigidity by 8×.
  4. Check spindle speed — a small RPM change can find a stable "sweet spot."

Verification

After optimization, always: dry-run at reduced feed, check the first piece, and record what changed. Document the new parameters so the next setup benefits.

Practice

1. A tool change takes 8 seconds and the program has 12 tools. If you can combine two operations into one tool, how much time per cycle do you save?

Show answer

8 seconds (one fewer tool change). Across 100 parts, that is 800 seconds = 13 minutes.

2. Why does reducing radial width of cut often eliminate chatter better than reducing speed?

Show answer

Chatter comes from the force per tooth exciting a natural frequency. Narrowing ae reduces the cutting force amplitude directly; changing RPM shifts the frequency but may hit another resonance.
