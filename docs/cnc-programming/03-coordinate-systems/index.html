Coordinate Systems and Positioning

Understanding coordinates is the single most important foundational skill in CNC programming. This page covers Cartesian coordinates, machine vs work offsets, and absolute vs incremental motion.

Concept

A point in space is defined by its X, Y, Z coordinates relative to an origin. The Cartesian coordinate system uses three perpendicular axes: X (left-right), Y (in-out), Z (up-down). Positive and negative directions follow the right-hand rule.

Machine coordinate system has its origin at machine zero (reference position). Work coordinate system (G54) has its origin at the workpiece datum. The machine always knows its position in machine coordinates; the work offset tells it where the part is.

Why It Matters

If the work offset is wrong by 2 mm in X, every hole and edge on the part shifts 2 mm. The program may look perfect on screen but the part is scrap. Coordinate errors are systematic — they affect every feature, not just one.

How It Works

Absolute vs Incremental

G90 (absolute): every coordinate refers to the work zero. G01 X50 means "go to X50." G91 (incremental): every coordinate refers to the current position. G01 X50 means "move 50 mm in X."

Example: starting at X0, G90 G01 X50 ends at X50. Starting at X20, G91 G01 X50 ends at X70. The same numbers produce different results depending on the mode.

G53 — Machine Coordinates

G53 switches to machine coordinates for one block only. It is used for machine-specific positions like tool change or pallet change, not for part programming. It is not modal.

Example

Start at (0,0). Absolute: G90 G01 X50 Y20 goes to (50,20). Incremental from (0,0): G91 G01 X50 Y20 also goes to (50,20). But from (10,10): absolute goes to (50,20); incremental goes to (60,30). Always know which mode you are in.

Machine-to-work conversion: if machine zero is at W=(-300,-200) and the part point is P=(40,30) in work coordinates, the machine position is (-260,-170). Machine = W + P.

Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting to restore G90 after a G91 subroutine — subsequent absolute moves become incremental and accumulate errors.
  • Treating G53 as modal — it is non-modal and active only for the block it is in.
  • Ignoring rotation/scaling — if the work plane is rotated or scaled, simple translation formulas no longer apply.

Practice

1. Point A is (5,8), point B is (-5,18). What are ΔX and ΔY?

Show answer

ΔX = -5 - 5 = -10 mm. ΔY = 18 - 8 = +10 mm.

2. In G91 mode from X20, you write X30. Where do you end up? Where would you be in G90?

Show answer

G91: X20 + 30 = X50. G90: X30. The same number means a different target.

3. Machine zero is at (-300,-200). A hole is at (20,10) in work coordinates. What is its machine position?

Show answer

(-300+20, -200+10) = (-280, -190).
