Tapping & Thread Milling

Tapping cuts internal threads. This page covers rigid tapping G84, tap drill sizing, and when thread milling is better.

Concept

Tapping uses a tap to cut internal threads in one operation. Rigid tapping (G84) synchronizes spindle rotation and Z feed so the tap follows the thread pitch exactly. Thread milling uses a rotating tool that helically interpolates around a bore — more flexible but more complex.

Why It Matters

Wrong tap drill size makes threads too shallow or too deep. Wrong feed in G84 strips the thread because feed must equal pitch. Tapping a blind hole requires bottoming tap and proper depth.

How It Works

G84 Rigid Tapping

G84 R5. Z-20. F=P×S

F = pitch × spindle speed. For M10×1.5 at S500: F = 1.5 × 500 = 750 mm/min.

Tap Drill Size

Tap drill = major diameter − pitch. M10×1.5: drill = 10 − 1.5 = 8.5 mm.

Example

M10×1.5 blind hole, 15 mm deep. Tap drill O8.5. Drill to Z-16.5 (15 + 1.5 tip). Tap to Z-15 (full thread depth). G84 at S500 F750.

Common Mistakes

  • Feed not tied to pitch — F must = P × S. Wrong feed strips threads.
  • Wrong tap drill size — use tap drill = major − pitch.
  • Blind hole with taper tap — use bottoming tap for full depth.

Practice

1. M8×1.25 at S600. G84 feed?

Ans

1.25 × 600 = 750 mm/min.

2. M12×1.75 tap drill size?

Ans

12 − 1.75 = 10.25 mm.

3. When thread mill over tap?

Ans

Large threads, non-ferrous materials, or when one tool must cut multiple thread sizes.
