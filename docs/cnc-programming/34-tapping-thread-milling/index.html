Tapping and Thread Milling

Tapping cuts internal threads with a tap. Thread milling uses a helical-interpolating cutter. They suit different hole sizes and materials.

What you will learn:
  • G84 rigid tapping cycle and synchronous feed
  • Why feed override must not be touched during tapping
  • Thread milling: helical interpolation, any pitch on any diameter
  • When to choose tapping vs thread milling

Rigid Tapping (G84)

G84 feeds a tap down at synchronized spindle speed and feed, then reverses spindle and retracts. The feed in mm/rev must equal the thread pitch. Do not use feed override during G84 — it breaks the tap. This is a synchronous operation.

Thread Milling

A thread mill is a solid carbide cutter that moves in a helical path: one full circle in X/Y while moving one pitch in Z. Advantages: one cutter works for any diameter (within range), produces chips instead of tapping debris, and is easier on hard materials. Requires helical interpolation capability.

Choosing

  • Tapping: simple, small holes, soft materials, short cycle time.
  • Thread milling: large holes, hard materials, good chip control, one tool for multiple sizes.

Common Mistakes

  • Using feed override during G84 — breaks the tap.
  • Choosing the wrong tap drill size — minor diameter must match the thread spec.
  • Thread milling without understanding helical interpolation (G02/G03 with Z movement).

Practice

1. Why can you not use feed override during G84?

Show answer

G84 is synchronous: spindle RPM and feed are locked together to maintain pitch. Changing feed breaks the thread form and likely the tap.

2. What is one advantage of thread milling over tapping?

Show answer

One thread mill cutter works for multiple diameters within its range, and chips are small and controlled — good for hard materials.
