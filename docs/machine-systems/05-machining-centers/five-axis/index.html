05.2 · Machining Centers

5-Axis Machining Centers

5-axis machines move the tool (or the part) in two additional rotary directions, so the tool can approach the part at any angle. This unlocks complex geometry and eliminates setups — but it's expensive and needs serious CAM.

Two Rotary Configurations

TypeRotary motionTypical use
Trunnion / TablePart tilts and rotates on an A/C trunnion tableSmall-to-medium parts, molds, aerospace
Head / SpindleSpindle head tilts (A/B), table stays flatLarge parts, heavy work, gantry-style

Why 5-Axis

  • One setup instead of five: Access all sides without re-fixturing.
  • Shorter, stiffer tools: Tilt the spindle normal to the surface — use short tools instead of long reach tools, reducing deflection and chatter.
  • Complex geometry: Impellers, turbine blades, tire molds, dental implants.

5-axis is not a magic upgrade. It needs (1) expensive machine, (2) sophisticated CAM software, (3) collision checking, (4) skilled programmers. For a simple block with holes, a 3-axis VMC is faster and more accurate. Buy 5-axis when the geometry demands it, not because it sounds impressive.

3+2 vs Full 5-Axis

3+2 (Positional)Full 5-Axis (Simultaneous)
How it worksIndex to angle, then mill in 3 axesAll 5 axes move continuously during cut
ProgrammingMuch simplerComplex CAM, collision checking
Use caseMachining sides at anglesFreeform surfaces, impellers, blades
