06.1 · Grinding Machines

Grinding Fundamentals

Grinding uses an abrasive wheel to remove tiny amounts of material — typically 0.01–0.05 mm per pass. It's the process that turns a turned or milled part into a precision part: tight tolerances, fine surface finish, and hardened materials.

Why Grind?

  • Tolerance: Grinding holds ±0.002–0.005 mm, better than turning or milling.
  • Surface finish: Ra 0.1–0.4 µm is typical.
  • Hardened materials: You grind hardened steel (50–65 HRC) after heat treat — turning can't cut it.

Types of Grinders

GrinderWhat it doesTypical parts
Surface grinderFlat horizontal surfaceDie sets, tooling, flat plates
Cylindrical grinderOD of rotating part between centersShafts, pins, bearing journals
Internal grinderID of a holeBearing races, bushings
Centerless grinderPart rests on a blade, no centersHigh-volume pins, shafts, bars

Grinding leaves residual stresses and heat. Burn, chatter, and thermal damage are real problems. Use proper wheel selection, dress the wheel regularly, and use ample coolant. Never take a heavy cut — grinding is a finishing operation, not roughing.
